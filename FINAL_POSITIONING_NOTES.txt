FINAL RESEARCH POSITIONING
===========================

1. SEASONAL BASELINE
Single-site 2024 holdout:
S2 free-running RMSE: 0.622287
S2 assimilated RMSE: 0.494698
S2 reduction: 20.5%

S1 free-running RMSE: 0.603218
S1 assimilated RMSE: 0.596239
S1 reduction: 1.2%

Seasonal baseline:
S2 RMSE: 0.472660
S1 RMSE: 0.525874

Interpretation:
The assimilated digital twin improves upon its own free-running forecast,
but does not outperform the seasonal baseline in the single-site experiment.
Do NOT claim universal baseline superiority.

2. GLOBAL ASSIMILATION
S1-only improvement vs free-running: 15.07%
S2-only improvement vs free-running: 0.87%
S1+S2 improvement vs free-running: 16.00%

Interpretation:
The main contribution is sequential state correction through assimilation,
not universal superiority over a simple seasonal benchmark.

3. UNCERTAINTY
Overall spread-error Pearson correlation: r = 0.217

1-sigma empirical coverage: 27.89%
90% interval empirical coverage: 40.50%
2-sigma empirical coverage: 44.95%

Interpretation:
Ensemble dispersion is an informative but imperfect relative uncertainty
signal. It is NOT calibrated probabilistic uncertainty.

Manuscript wording:
"Ensemble dispersion provided an informative but imperfect indicator of
predictive uncertainty. Forecast spread showed a modest positive association
with absolute error (Pearson r = 0.217), and observations in the highest-spread
quartile exhibited substantially larger mean absolute errors than those in the
lowest-spread quartile. However, empirical interval coverage was substantially
below nominal coverage, indicating that the current ensemble uncertainty is
not calibrated. We therefore interpret ensemble spread as a relative
uncertainty signal rather than a calibrated probabilistic interval."

Future work:
Calibrate uncertainty using out-of-sample calibration or conformal prediction.
